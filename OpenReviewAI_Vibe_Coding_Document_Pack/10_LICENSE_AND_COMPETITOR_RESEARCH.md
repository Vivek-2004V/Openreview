# License, Reverse Engineering, and Competitor Research

## 1. Research objective

Study publicly observable workflows and documented features to design an
independent code-review product. This is product research, not a plan to
obtain proprietary source code or bypass access controls.

## 2. REA

The public REA repository describes an agent-oriented
reverse-engineering tool and states that REA-authored code is MIT
licensed, while third-party material retains its own licenses and
notices. Verify the current upstream repository, exact commit/tag,
license files, package contents, and third-party notices before reuse.

Official repository: https://github.com/morluto/rea

Do not assume that every external tool integrated by REA is free or
redistributable. Commercial tools and their trials are subject to their
own license terms.

## 3. Competitor research method

For CodeRabbit and other code-review products, use: - public
documentation, - public pricing and plan pages, - public demos and
screenshots, - your own authorized test repositories/accounts, -
published product announcements and changelogs.

Record observations in a table: - feature/workflow, - public source
URL, - observed behavior, - confidence (documented / observed /
inferred), - independent implementation idea, - legal/licensing
concern, - validation test.

Do not: - access private endpoints without authorization, - bypass
authentication, paywalls, or technical restrictions, - scrape private
user data, - copy proprietary source, prompts, branding, or protected
assets, - represent your product as an official or affiliated CodeRabbit
product.

## 4. Product benchmark dimensions

-   Review triggers and scope.
-   Changed-line comments and summary reports.
-   Severity/risk categorization.
-   Rules/configuration.
-   Context selection.
-   Security analysis.
-   Suggested fixes.
-   Test evidence.
-   Noise/false positives.
-   Privacy and data retention.
-   Local/self-hosted options.
-   Cost and usage limits.

## 5. Open-source compliance checklist

-   [ ] Record upstream URL, commit/tag, and retrieval date.
-   [ ] Preserve required copyright and license notices.
-   [ ] Inventory direct and transitive dependencies.
-   [ ] Check licenses for static scanners, model runtimes, UI
    libraries, and bundled assets.
-   [ ] Keep third-party notices in distributions where required.
-   [ ] Do not use product names/logos in a way that implies
    endorsement.
-   [ ] Ask qualified legal counsel for material commercial
    redistribution questions.

This document is a practical checklist, not legal advice.
